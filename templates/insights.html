Scientific Finding: Why Ambient Weather Was Uncorrelated

During initial exploration, historical daily and hourly weather data from Open-Meteo was joined to test hypotheses regarding thermal load and cooling demand. However, correlation analysis revealed that temperature, humidity, and rainfall each exhibited Pearson coefficients of less than 0.04 with monthly electricity hours.

A deep dive into appliance logs explained this phenomenon: household Air Conditioner runtime remained virtually static at an average of 1.50 ± 0.02 hours/day across all 12 months (from 1.48h in May to 1.53h in September). Because users in the dataset did not systematically increase AC hours during summer peaks, weather features possessed negligible predictive signal. SPARK maintains scientific transparency by reporting these empirical results rather than fabricating artificial weather effects.
